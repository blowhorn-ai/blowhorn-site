Add a profile

Create a profile from the desktop app or the command line.

A profile is one voice you post as: a person in your organization, or a brand that represents nobody. Create one from the desktop app or from the command line.

Early access: profiles live in your organization in your Blowhorn account, and customer onboarding for that account is not open yet. This page stays a draft until it is.

A profile has three things you set: its name, what you type after --profile; an optional description; and who it represents. Its accounts on each platform are not part of the form: Google Chrome profile discovery finds the ones you are signed in to, and each platform card connects the rest. What a profile holds is in the Profile reference.

Before you start

  • Sign in and choose your organization (Sign in). With your account unreachable the create is refused (exit 3) and nothing is written.
  • The person the profile represents must be a member of your organization. Invite them to your organization in Blowhorn first, or create the profile as a brand.

From the desktop app

  1. Open Profiles and click add profile.
  2. Type the name: lowercase letters, digits, - or _. Add a description if you like.
  3. Under who is this profile?, choose you, someone in your organization and pick the member, or nobody · a brand.
  4. Click create profile.

Then give the profile its accounts: open the profile and choose its Browser tab. It lists the profile's own Chrome profile first, then the Chrome profiles no other profile uses yet, and says No Chrome profile yet when none is mapped. Turn one on to map it and attach the accounts signed in there. Each card counts the accounts the profile uses there, connected ones included even when Chrome shows no sign-in for them. Use a platform card for anything else, such as a GitHub token.

The profile's header states its worst problem in the list's own words, such as Not signed in · authenticate or Chrome profile changed, and reads Ready only when nothing needs you. The Checked column says how long ago the last session check ran; hover it for the exact date and time.

A profile you create for someone else is listed for them too, so their own Blowhorn shows it once they sign in to your organization. They can read it but not change it: editing a profile takes its ownership or the Edit Profile permission in your organization. The accounts linked to it are read only by its owner and by people who can view your organization's profiles, so the person it represents sees the profile without its accounts unless they hold that permission.

Your organization's profiles and members are read from your Blowhorn account. When either read fails, the screen names the failure where it would have listed them, rather than show an empty list. The Add form's member picker says can't read your organization's members. Every other profile picker says can't read your organization's profiles: the routine and post editors in Activity, the LinkedIn admin picker in Performance, the excluded profiles in Settings, and Diagnostics.

Edit a profile

Select the profile on Profiles. Its name, description and who is this profile? are editable; the represented person's name and email are shown from their member record and are changed in your Blowhorn account, not here. Click save. Renaming a profile changes what you type after --profile, and any routine or excluded-profiles entry that names the old name needs the new one. Its Chrome mapping and its content items follow the profile, because they hold its id.

Delete a profile

From the desktop app, select the profile and click delete profile. From a terminal:

Name it by its id instead when two profiles in your organization share a name, as blowhorn profile list prints it. Only the profile with that id is deleted:

The profile is deleted in your organization and unlinked from its accounts; the accounts themselves are kept. With your account unreachable the delete is refused and nothing changes.
