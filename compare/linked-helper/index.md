# Blowhorn vs Linked Helper

> Blowhorn isn't a bot. It's you. Linked Helper wins at deep, desktop LinkedIn prospecting, so if LinkedIn is the whole job and you like its depth, pick it. Blowhorn is for careful outreach and posts that earn real replies, across many networks, from a fleet of your computers.

## Choose Linked Helper if

- You want deep LinkedIn automation on the desktop, including Sales Navigator, Recruiter and Talent
- A built-in CRM, data export, endorsements and profile visits are part of your routine
- AI-written messages and comments, checked against your ideal customer, appeal to you
- A low per-account price on a long license suits you

[Linked Helper](https://www.linkedhelper.com/)

## Choose Blowhorn if

- You want careful LinkedIn outreach, not volume prospecting
- No vendor server logs in as you. Blowhorn acts from your own signed-in browser, on your own computers
- You also speak on X, Reddit, Hacker News, GitHub, Bluesky and Slack, and you want your people amplifying your posts as themselves
- You are a solo founder, a local practice, a big team or an agency, and you want one queue for every profile

## Strong signal, not bought numbers

Blowhorn doesn't sell followers, likes or repost packages, and it never will. A like from someone paid to click is weak signal: it moves a number and nothing else. Strong signal is a reply, a question, a repost with a real take from someone who knows your work, a thread that turns into a discussion. Blowhorn exists to get your message in front of the people who would answer it, through the profiles of real people who chose to back you.

Automating more invites and visits gets more numbers. Blowhorn caps itself and spends the effort on posts real people want to answer.

## Frequently Asked Questions {#questions}

**Is Linked Helper better than Blowhorn?** For deep, desktop LinkedIn prospecting, yes. For careful outreach plus multi-network publishing and amplification, we think Blowhorn is the better fit.

**Does Linked Helper keep working when my computer sleeps?** On its local version, no: the vendor says the computer has to be on and running the app, unless you run it on a server you control. Blowhorn has the same rule in another shape: it needs at least one of your computers awake.
