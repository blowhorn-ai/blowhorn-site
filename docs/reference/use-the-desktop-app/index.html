The Blowhorn app, screen by screen

The menu-bar popover, the console screens, Setup, Settings, and what each one does.

What each part of the Blowhorn desktop app shows and does. This page describes; the steps live in the how-to guides linked under Related.

The menu bar and the popover

Blowhorn lives in the menu bar. Opening it shows the popover, top to bottom:

  • The header: the Blowhorn mark and wordmark (not a link), a ? that says what the popover is for, Settings and Quit.
  • The scheduler line, under the header when a check has been recorded: Working now and what it is checking, while a check is running, or Last check and when that one finished, when it is idle. Home's Today shows the same line. It stays in the ordinary text colour. It never shows a process id, a file path or the raw status.
  • Three stats: how many items need you (gold above zero), runs done in the last seven days, and the success share over the same week. A dash means no runs or no reading.
  • The create button: New order, or New activity when Major Blowhorn is off. Under it, one line says you can drop a link, image or video anywhere on the popover.
  • NEXT UP: the next three due items. open › opens one in Activity. When nothing is due, the popover says so.
  • OFF DUTY, in place of NEXT UP while this computer is off duty, whether by its own switch, the fleet's or a stop: how many items wait, what holds this computer (this computer, the fleet, quiet hours and a stop, each with who, when and why), and the first three waiting items. N more › opens the rest in Activity. It shows as soon as a switch flips, and NEXT UP comes back when you reenlist.
  • RECENTLY DONE: the three newest posts that went out in the last seven days, newest first, from the Content queue (its Sent tab). ↗ opens the item's proof link in Google Chrome. An item with no proof link opens in Content. Until the popover has read the queue it shows a placeholder line, and if the read fails it says it can't read what went out, never that nothing went out. Home's Recent activity lists every run Blowhorn recorded, so it can show runs that have no post in the queue behind them.
  • Duty: This computer › and Fleet ›, each with its switch. A switch shows its state by its knob alone. The name opens that screen.
  • Open console, and, when there is one, an update card.

A notice line sits under the header when something needs you: a store outage, a fleet state that cannot be read, or a refused pause. The popover never offers Check now, a countdown or a timer. Its stats and lists refresh when the popover opens and when main pushes a change.

Stop everything is not on the popover. Use the console or blowhorn stop --all for it.

Drop a link, image or video

Drop anything on the popover, with no prompt. What you drop decides the sheet:

  • A link to a post or comment on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Bluesky, Slack or GitHub opens the engage sheet for it. The sheet offers the verbs that platform takes: Respond and Amplify on LinkedIn, Reddit and Bluesky, Amplify alone on X and GitHub, and Respond alone on Slack. A verb the platform cannot do is left out.
  • Only profiles with an active account on that platform are offered. The one you used last on that platform is selected, and that choice is remembered on this computer. A platform with no active profile says so in one line and links to Profiles.
  • Any other link, including a Hacker News item, starts a new order that uses the page's title and summary.
  • An image or video starts a new order.
  • Anything else is refused with the line "Links, images or video only" (in the military voice, "Wrong intel").

Respond or Amplify writes an approved item, due now, for each profile you select. For 10 seconds afterwards the sheet offers Undo, which removes what it wrote. After that the order is final.

New order asks the Blowhorn extension in Google Chrome for the page in front. It retries briefly and never uses Apple Events, so it never raises a macOS Automation prompt. An open post opens the engage sheet, and an open page starts a new order from that page. If the live read misses but the extension answers, New order can use the last remembered normal-window page and shows From your last Chrome tab: title with Change. Change opens the message field containing the page details.

If the extension does not answer, New order still opens without a page. Every kind stays selectable and drafts can be saved. When a kind needs the extension, the kind and final step say This order needs the Blowhorn extension in Google Chrome before it can run. The kind step also says Some orders need the Blowhorn extension in Google Chrome. Choose Install the extension to install it, or Try again to check whether it is available. API-backed kinds do not show this prerequisite.

When an update is ready, the update card at the bottom of the popover offers Update. The update downloads the new version, installs it and relaunches the app; when it cannot, it says why and offers the release page. In the console, an update shows its progress over the whole window until Blowhorn reopens. An installed Blowhorn that finds such a release when it opens also asks in the console window, before the download, with Update now, Later and a ? help icon; what each does is under Update. With Sounds on in Settings, a new version gets a short announcement when the prompt first shows, the update plays quiet music while it downloads and installs, and a finished update plays a short chime: on the new version's first launch when Blowhorn relaunched to finish. Turning Sounds off silences all three. The update dialog itself carries a speaker toggle at the top right beside its title; it is the same Sounds setting as in Settings, so switching it off mid-update silences the update music at once, and switching it on again restores it.

The console

The console is the full window, reached from the popover. The Blowhorn logo at the top of its rail is Home; there is no separate Home item. Below it are four places: Activity, Profiles, Performance and Posture. The round + button in the lower right of every screen, teal glass unless you change the material, is New order (⌘N, or New order in the File menu), or New activity when Major Blowhorn is off. It opens a field order you fill in one step at a time. Only the open step shows its fields; a finished step folds to a one-line summary (press it to change it), and the steps ahead wait as dim rows. A step that holds what it asks for takes a teal check; one you passed empty, such as a skipped landing zone, takes a dashed "-". Each step's Next moves on.

Press and hold the + button for about a third of a second (or press Up arrow while it has focus) to fan out shortcuts above it. A click, Enter or Space still opens a blank order. The fan lists the three kinds of order (Compose a dispatch, Answer the line, Rally the troops), then a mark for each platform you have connected. A kind opens the order on Dispatch to with that kind ready for when you pick a platform. A platform opens it with that platform picked and From the desk of open. Esc, or a press anywhere else, closes the fan and opens nothing. With reduced motion on, the shortcuts are the same and nothing animates.

  1. Dispatch to - press one or more platform tiles (LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Bluesky, Slack, GitHub), each showing that platform's mark in its own colour; a picked tile lifts and rings in that colour. Press again to drop one. Then Orders that fit: Compose a dispatch (a post), Answer the line (a reply to a link) or Rally the troops (amplify a link). Only the orders every picked platform takes are offered; with GitHub picked, that is Rally alone. Each order has an info button on its top-right corner, shown when you point at the order: its tip says what the order does on each platform picked, and links to that platform's supported actions and to the action-by-platform matrix (the platforms page). A ? beside Orders that fit opens the field manual, a drawer with each order in plain words and what it does on each platform picked, with the same links; Esc closes it and returns focus to the ?. Beside them, Or run lists every other action the picked platforms can run once, in one select: on LinkedIn Invite to a page, Accept invitations, Withdraw stale invitations and Collect analytics; on X and Bluesky Follow accounts and Collect analytics; on GitHub Follow accounts. With several platforms picked it lists the actions all of them run. The list is the same catalog the command line schedules from, so an action Blowhorn can schedule is never missing here. Its info button describes every action until you choose one, then the one you chose.
  2. From the desk of - one or more profiles. Each desk sends its own copy to every platform, so two platforms from two desks is four posts.
  3. The message, with its character count (and, for a reply or a rally, the link it answers or amplifies). Type @ to tag a person, company, page or community: a note says what the tag means, and the tags found show as chips. Each platform may know them by a different name, so Next first opens Who's who: for each tag, one line per platform picked, prefilled with a guess in that platform's form - @handle on X, @name.bsky.social on Bluesky, u/ or r/ on Reddit, a member id (U0…) on Slack. LinkedIn can't carry tags yet, and the order says so: its copy names each one as plain text, without the @, and nothing on LinkedIn is tagged. Blowhorn looks none of them up here, so check each one, edit any that are wrong, and leave a line empty to send that platform the name as plain text. Looks right moves on; change the message or the platforms afterwards and the order asks again before it can deploy. Each platform's post carries the message with its own form of each tag. Your answers are not remembered for the next order yet. A composed dispatch also offers Single dispatch | Radio net. A radio net is a conversation between profiles: the first transmission opens the net, and each one after it is a reply from one of the profiles you picked to the transmission above it, with its own words and character count, closed with "Over." + Add a reply offers the next profile, each reply's profile can be switched, and a reply's × stands it down. Replies go out a random number of minutes apart within the window you pick (2-6 by default), never seconds apart; GitHub only rallies, so it sits a net out. The manifest shows the chain (K › L › K) and Deploy counts platforms x transmissions. A radio net cannot go out yet: each reply has to answer the post the transmission above it actually produced, read back from the platform, and your Blowhorn account cannot yet keep which item a reply answers. Until it can, Deploy, Hold in reserve and Dry fire wait, and the line above them says why. Switching back to Single dispatch keeps the opening transmission's words as the message. For an action this step is The details: the options that action takes, such as how many days old an invitation must be, how many to withdraw and apply for Withdraw stale invitations (without apply, the run only previews). A box left empty takes the command's own default, shown in it. An option a run cannot do without, such as the account to start from for Follow accounts, is marked required, and the order cannot go out until it is filled. Each option has an info button with what it means and the command's own help for it.
  4. Landing zone - drop one or more images or videos, or paste a link to one. Anything else is turned away with "Wrong intel - image or video only", and each landed file has its own Remove button. Blowhorn posts media from a link, so a dropped file needs its link pasted too. The zone takes no more drops than the strictest platform you picked allows: with Reddit picked, one drop only, and a dropped folder lands its first file and says so. Removing a landed file frees its place, so swapping stays possible. LZ clear moves on with no media. A rally or an action carries no media.
  5. When to march - On my mark (goes out now), or a date and a start time from the pickers. Time is read the military way ("1430 HRS"), with the day's bugle calls (Reveille 0600, Mess call 1200, Retreat 1700, Taps 2200) as quick picks; Roger ✓ closes the clock and Lock it in folds the order. An order goes out once: Repeat it? lists the standing orders (every day, weekday, week or month), and picking one opens the routine editor on the platform and desks you picked. A routine runs on one platform, so with more than one picked it asks you to pick one instead. The routine editor is where a repeating order lives, as do Hacker News, an organization blog (offered only when a profile in your organization is opted in to it, blowhorn schedule destinations) and a report.

On a wide enough window a narrow manifest slides out beside the order the first time there is something to show: the platforms and desks stacked like cards, the message's length (never its words), what landed, and when it goes: a field watch shows a set time, and a jump light stands for On my mark. When a picked platform has a character limit on record, the message's length carries a meter with a tick at the tightest such limit, and says by how much it runs over. On a narrow window the folded summaries say the same. The line above the buttons says what is still missing, or when the order goes out.

Deploy (it names the count, for example Deploy 4; a single post is just Deploy) adds one post to the queue for each platform and desk, with the same words, link, media and time. The steps fold away and the debrief takes the window: Orders deployed, one row per platform with how many were queued and when, and "link pending" until Blowhorn reads each post's own link back - never a guess. Debrief opens Performance filtered to just the posts this order wrote: a these dispatches chip in place of the filters, counting the posts the order wrote (its × puts the whole screen back), and one row per post with its profile, platform, status and when it is due or was sent (on your computer's clock, as "Mon, Jul 27, 2026 · 10:00 AM"). A post deleted from the queue since is counted under the rows ("1 of 4 dispatches is no longer in your queue"), so the rows and that line add up to the chip. Once its link is read back and the platform reports on it, its engagement and reach, labelled measured with the date it was read. A post whose link is not read back yet says link pending - there is nothing to measure, so it never shows a zero. The rows are read when Performance opens and again on the screen's 60-second poll, so each link and its reception appear as they are read back, with no leaving the screen. If any platform refuses, the card is an after-action report instead: Mission failure, how many platforms deployed, each refusal in its own words, and per platform Re-engage (try only what did not land) or Withdraw (leave it unsent). What landed is never written twice. An action deploys the same way, as one scheduled run per platform and desk that runs once, at the time you picked or now; its debrief rows have no link to wait for and no Debrief link, since an action is not a post with engagement to measure. The app checks an action in the window and again before it is saved, so one missing a required option or naming an option the command does not take is refused with the reason, never saved to fail later. Close then opens Activity for a post, or Activity filtered to recurring routines on the first action saved. Dry fire rehearses the order before you save it: for each platform and desk in turn it opens that profile's browser, checks the sign-in and stops before composing (Slack, Bluesky and GitHub post over their APIs, so they open no browser and stop before sending), listing each as it goes and ending "Nothing was posted." It saves nothing - no post, routine or record - and a platform it cannot walk says why on its own row. Hacker News is not offered, because even its dry run signs in. Dry fire is offered for posts only: an action has no post to rehearse. Hold in reserve saves the same posts unapproved instead, so nothing goes out until someone approves them in Activity (an action is saved turned off, filtered to recurring routines, and runs only once someone turns it on); Cancel closes the order without saving. The speaker button in the header beside the title starts in your UI sounds setting; turning it off mutes this order's sounds, and those of every post it saves. Turning it back on plays a short call from the sound family picked in Settings under Sound genre. An activity already saved for a blog still shows and runs. Activity is one place. Filters opens a drawer: time (Past and Upcoming, each on or off), recurrence (Any, Once or Recurring), status and a date range. Each applied filter shows once as a chip beside Filters. List and Calendar choose how that same set is shown. Day, week and month appear only while Calendar is selected. A deep link or a visit from Home seeds the filters (for example Past only for History); clicking Activity again on the rail keeps what you set. Past on with Upcoming off shows History in List or Calendar, because Calendar has no run events yet. Posture holds every rules-of-engagement choice and every limit Blowhorn keeps.

At the bottom of the rail, once you are signed in, one duty switch covers this computer: On duty, or Off duty while it is held. Its position and colour show the state, and it never names the action. Press it (or ⌘⇧P) to change it; it never touches the fleet. Under the fleet's leave alone it reads Off duty and is held: the fleet's switch on Home's Fleet card is the one that changes it. When the service is stopped, not installed or turned off in System Settings it still reads On duty, and a line above it gives the reason and opens This computer; the same line says when a check is late or when the fleet's state can't be read. Hover the switch for the next scheduled run (for example "Next: Post on X, tomorrow at 9:00 AM") or when the leave began. When the service runs from another copy of Blowhorn (a dev checkout, or a moved or older app), the line above the switch says so, and This computer names that copy in one sentence with its one fix, Register the service, which registers the service to this app. Under the switch, the account button (desktop v1.27.2) shows who is signed in: your Blowhorn profile photo, or your initials, your name, and your organization and plan (for example "Acme · Free plan"). The organization is the one your Blowhorn session has selected, the same one cloud.blowhorn.ai shows, unless BLOWHORN_CLOUD_ORGANIZATION names another; the plan is that organization's. Every organization is on the Free plan unless an active subscription names another. It opens the account menu: your name, email, organization and plan, then Your account and plan (your Blowhorn account page at cloud.blowhorn.ai, in your browser), Team (coming soon), Switch organization when you belong to more than one (it lists them inline in this menu with yours checked, and pointing at one, or moving to it with the arrow keys, shows its description and its ID, so two organizations with the same name can be told apart; picking one switches your account to it right here, the same switch cloud.blowhorn.ai makes, so your browser follows too; blowhorn auth switch-organization does it from Terminal), Settings (⌘,), Help and docs, and Sign out. With many organizations the list scrolls on its own, so your name at the top and Sign out at the bottom stay in view, and with more than eight a Filter organizations box above the list narrows it as you type (press the down arrow to move from the box into the list, and the up arrow on the list's first organization to go back to the box). The menu works from the keyboard: the up and down arrows move through it and wrap around at the ends, Home and End jump to the first and last item, typing a name's first letters jumps to it, Return or Space chooses the highlighted item, and Esc closes the menu. Sign out signs you out and takes you straight to the sign-in screen; if it can't, the menu's error line says why and you stay where you were. Opening the menu refreshes your name, photo, organizations and plan from your Blowhorn account. Signed out, the rail shows no sign-in button and no account placeholder: the guided setup's own sign-in buttons are the way in, and this computer's status line stays hidden. While Blowhorn checks your account it shows a spinner; when it can't reach Blowhorn it shows a warning dot, and its menu offers Try again. If Blowhorn refuses your sign-in, the error where it appears offers sign in again, which signs you back in from the app.

Each screen reads live state on open. Nothing here edits on a timer. Until you're signed in and the extension is ready, every place but Home, and New activity, is locked. Once you are signed in, the account button stays usable. See Set up Blowhorn at first muster.

Screen Shows Acts
Home (two tabs: Today and Resources) Today: whether Blowhorn is working and whether it needs you, but never what runs next: an activity's next run is on Activity with Recurring selected, and the rail's duty toggle names the next one on hover; the Fleet card: computers known (this one and every other computer that has run here or reported lately), how many are on duty or off duty, fleet holds, who took the fleet off duty and why, this computer's row, and a row for each other computer saying On duty, Off duty or Last pass failed from the last pass its background service reported, or Not reporting when it has reported nothing in the last half hour; a computer that is not reporting is never counted on duty, and the card says 2 of 3 reporting while any one is not; "Your draft was saved" once after setup, until you dismiss it or leave Home; beside them your results, and one card with Recent activity and What went wrong (it opens on what went wrong while something failed). Resources: ideas worth a try, the latest from Blowhorn - each piece labelled with its column, From the Desk of the General, From Boot Camp or From the Field - and the tips as a taught sequence: the next tip you have not seen yet, with its video, one at a time; opening a tip or choosing Another tip moves you on, a tip you have seen never comes back, and when you have seen them all the card says so and points to Resources. A newer release is offered on a card at the top of the results column, Update to Blowhorn and the version the entire fleet's switch, which opens an optional reason with Save (takes leave with the reason) and Skip (takes leave with none) buttons (Escape closes it and takes nothing), or this computer's switch, on the Fleet card; open This computer from this computer's row; reconnect a signed-out account from the Needs you section at the top of Today: one full-row button per signed-out account, its platform's mark and Reconnect (naming the profile, Reconnect @handle, only where two signed-out accounts share a platform), which signs that account in again and reads Reconnecting… while it runs, and the ? beside the title says which computer they are signed out on and how many scheduled posts wait (a Bluesky app password or a GitHub token opens Profiles instead); a sign-in that does not finish leaves the row with a note underneath saying so; open an item's details from a row in Recent activity or What went wrong; start the update from its card (Blowhorn restarts to finish); Connect an account opens a step for your own profile: each platform, how to sign in to it in your Google Chrome profile, and Check again, with connected ones ticked; finish setting up
Activity, posts Your posts with their status - "ready", "scheduled", "partly sent", "sent", "draft", "needs you" - and clicked-but-unread ones, each with when it goes out: its own time, "next run" when the next run would take it, "not scheduled" when nothing will send it (a draft, or a post that needs you), and "-" once it has gone out; an opened post shows its result: delivery per profile and platform, reception where it was collected, and the follower change since. With recurrence left on Any, the schedule's routines follow below the posts, each labelled by what it does; a routine names no post, so clicking one opens that routine's details Click a post to open it in a dialog (a box over the screen), or add one; edit it there; clearing a clicked post's "Date Promoted" queues it again
Activity, routines Your routines as a day, week or month calendar, or as a list: each item's next time, its last time, and the times that would follow, dashed because they are estimates; a "now" row for items going out or waiting for the service to take them up; a dialog for one item, opened by clicking it in the list or on the calendar; a chooser of sample routines, each one ticked on only once its platform is connected. Calendar is the presentation toggle; day, week and month show only then Add an item with the + button or by clicking a free time (Schedule saves it); move an item's next time by dragging it; edit one item in place in its dialog (its changes save as you make them); put it On hold or back In effect from the state pill beside its title; switch its duty hold from the duty switch on its row or its calendar chip; Do it now or Delete it from its dialog; turn dry fire on in the header to rehearse up to the final click and post nothing (the dry run, --dry-run); add the sample routines you tick
Activity, history Everything Blowhorn did, newest first, reached with Past on and Upcoming off, with each one's detailed log under the log directory's scheduler/ folder; clicking a run opens it in a dialog over the full-width list: when it started, how it ended and why it stopped, and the log and its file behind more details. A post's own result (delivery, reception, the follower change) is in its details on Activity, not on the run open a run; "try again" on a failure
Profiles Every profile, its accounts and their state, and the Google Chrome profiles on this computer each one uses. Clicking a profile opens its window over the list: the header says what Blowhorn does for the person and what needs you, and three tabs hold the rest. Accounts lists each connected account, and an account that needs you reads Reconnect. Browser lists the Google Chrome profiles on this computer beside the accounts in the one you pick; each is switched on or off for Blowhorn, and Discover finds signed-in sessions again. About holds the name, the description, who the profile is for, and delete. Nothing has a Save button: switches and fields save as you change them Add a profile (in its own form); reconnect an account on Accounts; on Browser, turn a Google Chrome profile on or off, set up an account, or add a token or app password; change the name, description or who it is for, or delete the profile, on About; check all profiles on the list
Performance Followers, growth, and activity per profile; collection freshness in the header. Under the filters, three tabs that all read them: Activity (runs by platform, activity per profile), History (the figures, followers over time, new versus unfollowed per month) and Report (your LinkedIn pages and the health of each profile and page) Collect now; open the HTML report
Posture (three tabs: Rules, Limits, Protection; the fleet's Take leave and stop sit on the tabs' line) Rules: the rules of engagement for everyone (the slider across the card: Safety on, Weapons tight or Weapons free; beside Everyone, Custom when a limit is set outside the preset), which choice wins, one grid of every profile on every platform with where each cell's choice comes from. Limits, one row per section: by platform (pace, daily caps as sliders, and how runs overlap), speed and amount (spacing, outreach, how much runs at once), when and who says yes (quiet hours, approval, dry fire), typing, and pointer timing; each row opens its settings in a window. Protection, one row per section: what happens when a platform pushes back, what the last check held back, where your sign-ins live and what Blowhorn will never do Move the slider; choose a preset for one platform for every profile, one profile on every platform, or one cell, or reset any of them to inherit; reset them to the preset; each limit saves as you change it; take leave or stop every computer; run a held activity once with Do it anyway; turn the sign-in check, the needs-you note, dry fire by default and failure reports on or off (while dry fire is on, Approve and every duty switch, a routine's own included, each say so in one line, because nothing was approved or changed)
This computer (from Home's computer row, or the rail's reason line) Status: whether this computer is on duty and, when it is not, the one sentence on what is wrong; one This computer card with the background service's state and who registered it (in red when it is anything but this app), the last pass, the engine's version, and the Blowhorn extension in each Google Chrome profile (on, with its version, or not added); the schedule card counts the activities it checked (5 scheduled). When the service keeps exiting, its Service row names the likely cause in one line, with See how to fix it, which opens Troubleshooting. Troubleshooting: while the service keeps exiting, a card at the top says why, from the service's own log: the likely cause, what to do, the last line it logged and the error code for support; then who registered the service, its process, how it last exited, its command, the logs folder, the versions, and the service log's last lines Its one fix under the banner: "register the service" when the service is not registered or runs from another copy of Blowhorn, "start the service" when it is registered but not running, "open Login Items" when it is turned off in System Settings (only those states offer one); when it keeps exiting, See how to fix it on the Status tab, then each step in the Troubleshooting card that Blowhorn can take is its button (Register the service, Update Blowhorn, Open service log, Contact support), and after one the card says whether the service now runs or still exits; Open log and Show in Finder for the service log and the logs folder; "check now" (with "dry fire" to rehearse up to the final click, posting nothing); "take leave for the fleet", and the fleet's switch while the fleet holds
Settings (from the account menu; its pages are listed in the left rail under the places while it is open) General: appearance (theme, material, spacing), notifications (system notifications, Major Blowhorn, Sounds) and usage sharing. Background & updates: startup, keeping this computer on duty after quit, the pause shortcut, the update channel, uninstall. Setup: setup and the guided setup, the extension card. Advanced: app paths and the engine defaults Posture does not hold, rendered from the CLI's own option list Change defaults, check the extension, choose its mode; run setup again or quit or uninstall; Posture for pace, limits, and approval. Your account is not in Settings: the account menu holds Your account and plan, Switch organization and Sign out

The routines list keeps every value on one line, and it has the whole width of the screen: an item's details open in a dialog, not beside it. When the window is small, the browser and recurrence columns leave first (an item's dialog still shows both) and a long profile list is shortened with an ellipsis; the status is never cut. In the narrowest window the number leaves too, and each routine's duty switch shrinks to its track (its words stay in its name). Every row carries that switch beside its status: click it to take the routine off duty or put it back on duty, without opening the row. A list longer than the screen is shown a page at a time, as many rows as fit, with previous and next at its foot.

Clicking a routine, in the list or on the calendar, opens its details in a dialog (a box over the screen), all on one page with no tabs. Its title band shows the platform's mark, what it does and who for, and beside it the state pill: In effect, or On hold while the routine is turned off. Click the pill for Hold or Resume; while that saves, the pill says Holding… or Resuming…. Below are four rows, each with a short summary, that open in place when you click them, one at a time:

  • What says what it does, as in "Post as Kate on LinkedIn". Open it to change the command, platform, profiles, browser and parameters, and to Delete the routine.
  • When says how often and when it goes next, as a date. Click the date to pick a new next run on the calendar (Set moves it); open the row to change the time, the repeat and the retries. A routine held with its duty switch says who held it, when and why here, with Resume.
  • Rules says which rules of engagement it follows from Posture, or its own. Open it to choose its own; a choice looser than Posture's for that profile on that platform gets a note in coral.
  • Log says when it last went out, as a date. Open it for the last run's log (live while it runs, and it opens by itself then), with the troubleshooting facts (the exact call, the driver, the retries, the lock and the edit check) folded under it.

The footer holds Close on the left and Do it now on the right; Do it now says Going out… while it runs. Esc, Close or a click outside closes the dialog, and focus goes back to the item you clicked. A post's or a run's dialog shows its own facts, with the rest behind more details. A routine or post on Home, a routine under Activity's posts and a row in the menu-bar popover open the same dialog over its own screen; a run on Home opens its run dialog where you are.

The day and week calendars show all 24 hours at once, an hour a row; the rows grow when the window gives them room. The now lane shows three items and a "+N more".

The Upcoming list keeps each value on one line too, and the preview gives way first: in a small window "goes out" leaves the list (the post's dialog shows it), and below that the number and the preview.

The Upcoming list has the full width of the screen, so each post's preview is on screen at the default window size. Clicking a post opens it in a dialog, a box over the screen, with its words, when it goes out, whether it is approved, and what came of it; New order opens the same dialog on a new entry. The columns nobody reads daily sit behind more details. If you typed something and close the dialog, it asks before it throws your changes away.

The Routines view opens on the week calendar and remembers the layout you pick; opening a day from the month makes the day view the remembered one. The tabs ("coming up", "due now", "going out", "failed", "all") and the filter box choose which items it shows, in every layout. An item's next time is a fact; the times after it are drawn dashed, because Blowhorn schedules each one from when the last one finished, so they drift. An item that repeats more often than every hour shows its next time only (the month shows daily and slower). Moving one asks your account first and refuses if someone else changed that item since you loaded it. A finished item (it happened once, or gave up after its retries) shows its last time and no next one; giving it a new "when" in its editor schedules it again, with its full set of retries.

The Performance screen states how fresh its numbers are behind the ? in its header and has no refresh control of its own: it polls every 60 seconds, the debrief's rows included. Its activity heatmap has one row per profile; attempts recorded for several profiles at once (all, or a comma list) name no one profile, so they are counted beside it rather than shown as a row. The History view reaches each item's detailed log through the log directory listing, which includes each capture file and its rollover. The this computer screen is the visible face of blowhorn service: it reports the service the app registered and never installs, updates or uninstalls one.

The app lists each profile by the name your account holds and reads and saves it as the lowercase name every command resolves it to.

An empty screen says what it is for and what to do next, with one member of the cast beside the note. Every empty screen has one, including a filter that hides everything, a table, a form, a dropdown and the menu bar's queue. Two empty areas on one screen merge into one. Where the next step is a button on that screen, the button is there, and an info button beside it links the docs page for it. Where the next step is the + button or a button in the header, such as collect now on Performance, the note names it rather than repeating it. With Major Blowhorn off in Settings, the same note and action show without the art, and Major's line goes with them. A tab with nothing on it says what goes there and offers one button: Upcoming's points at the tab where your posts are, with its count (show drafts (1)), and History's at today or all, or at the + button when nothing has run yet. A filter that hides everything on a tab offers clear filter.

Upcoming opens on the first tab that holds a post or a routine - ready, needs you, drafts, scheduled, then sent - so right after guided setup it opens on your first post, often a draft. When the board holds only routines, it opens on scheduled, which lists them. A tab you pick, or one a link opens, stays put.

On the guided setup's steps, Major Blowhorn's portrait in the left rail is alive. His eyes and head follow your pointer, and now and then he looks around, pulls a face, polishes his bugle or gives a thumbs up. Click him and he sounds his bugle, double-click him and he lets Old Thunderlung rip; he only makes a sound when you click him, and only with Sounds on. Leave him alone and he points at the step's button, then turns into one of his oil paintings until you come back. With Major Blowhorn off in Settings the rail shows no portrait, and with Reduce motion on in your Mac's accessibility settings he holds still. The same setting hides the periscope picture on the Chrome side panel's idle screen; the line under it stays. The side panel follows the setting within a few seconds: no reload, and you do not need to close and open it again.

Setup

Setup is the engine checklist the app opens on while its engine is not ready (otherwise the first muster is the guided setup; see Set up Blowhorn at first muster), five rows read from this computer: the engine rows ("blowhorn engine" and "bundled engine" in the installed app; "Blowhorn folder" and "helper tools" in a source build), "organization connection", "chrome profiles", and "this computer on duty". The first two check the engine bundled in the app, the only engine the installed app runs (Install the app). Setup itself never clones or installs: a source build with no engine is told to clone the repository and run ./tools/install.sh in Terminal. An app run from its disk image says to move it to Applications instead. Each row reports ok, warn, or off with the one fix that clears it. The "map chrome profiles" panel and the "this computer on duty" row act directly: mapping a profile and registering the service happen here, not in a terminal.

Settings

Settings edits the same .blowhorn.yaml defaults the CLI reads: default profile and platform, excluded profiles, notifications, logs, and the Chrome launch mode. Nothing has a Save button: a switch saves as you flip it, and a text, number or path field saves when you leave it. A completed save shows one brief "Changes saved" notice in the lower-right corner; a change that could not be saved says Not saved on the card. Its appearance card is the app's own: the theme, the material of the side rail and the + button (Solid, Teal glass, Ink glass or Clear glass, with tint strength, see-through and reduce transparency, which is on by default), and spacing; see Change how the app looks. Pace, daily caps, quiet hours, how much runs at once, approval, dry fire and the rules of engagement are on the Posture screen instead; each Settings card that used to hold one says so and links there. The engine-defaults card renders from blowhorn config show, so the app can never offer an option the engine does not know. The extension card reports whether the extension is installed, which version answers, and whether it says connected, and sets its extension mode, classic or background, the Blowhorn tab group (collapsed or open) and where Blowhorn works (beside your tabs or in its own window, mostly behind yours), on your Blowhorn account. guided setup at the bottom reopens the first-muster setup, and run setup again opens Setup. Quitting the app leaves posting to the background service and the keep-running setting; see What runs when the app is closed.

Posture

Posture opens on its tabs, with Take leave for the fleet and Stop everything beside them; the rail names the screen, so it has no visible title.

  • Who runs how carefully. The slider sets the rules of engagement for everyone: Safety on (slow and careful, in teal blue), Weapons tight (normal pace, in teal) or Weapons free (fast, in coral); the CLI and the config file keep cautious, standard and brisk. Under it, which choice wins numbers the levels from everyone to one activity, and the grid has a row for each platform's choice for every profile (Each platform), a column for each profile's choice on every platform (Every platform), and a cell for one profile on one platform. A filled chip was chosen at that spot; a dashed chip is inherited, and the line under it says from where ("from LinkedIn", "from this profile", "from everyone"). A profile still warming up shows Warming and the preset it returns to. A chip in the Each platform row or the Every platform column opens a small slider, with Follows Everyone to reset it; when a platform and a profile both choose, the more careful one wins, and a profile's slider names each platform that wins over it. Any other chip opens a menu to choose a preset or Inherit, and a cell chosen here shows a reset beside it when you point at it or tab to it. A platform's choice is kept for every computer; when it could only be saved on this computer, the row says "this computer only". The order is in Which choice wins.
  • The limits Blowhorn keeps. One row per section (by platform, speed and amount, when and who says yes, typing, pointer timing). Each row opens its settings in a window. By platform picks one platform by its logo (only the platforms the rules of engagement cover), then that platform's pace and its daily caps as sliders that stop at the built-in ceiling. A choice saves as you make it, a cap slider saves when you let go of it, a typed value or a safety range saves when you leave the field or close the window, and Reset to preset puts the dials back.
  • When a platform pushes back. Each row opens a window holding the pause after a challenge, any sign-in that needs you (with open Profiles ›), how often sign-ins are checked, and the needs-you note.
  • Held back. What the last check held, why, and when it can run, with Do it anyway for a rules-of-engagement hold.
  • Where your sign-ins live and Blowhorn will never are the promises, each one row that opens its details in a window; the failure reports switch is in the sign-ins window.

Info buttons stay hidden until you point at their row or tab to them.
