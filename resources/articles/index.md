# Articles

Product intelligence, tips and field notes from the Blowhorn team: how to read your results, schedule well and stay inside every platform's rules.


- [Failed posts are data: reading failure reasons and fixing the cause](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/failed-posts-are-data/) - A failed post is a report, not just a red mark. The reason on the activity names the cause, and the fix follows from the reason: re-sign in, shrink the run, fix the content, or wait out the platform. Retry only after a fix, never in a loop.

- [Pace is a feature: typing delay, concurrency and approval gates](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/pace-is-a-feature/) - Speed feels like efficiency until a platform reads it as automation. Typing delay, concurrency and approval gates are the three dials that keep a fast schedule looking human, and each one trades a little time for a lot of safety.

- [The open web is closing. Show up where your audience is.](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/the-open-web-is-closing/) - AI answers now reuse Reddit's posts without sending the click, and Reddit is answering with logins and its app. Here is what that shift means for anyone who builds an audience in public, and how to keep showing up in your own voice.

- [Your release, drafted by your assistant, fired by you](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/your-release-drafted-by-your-assistant/) - Your changelog already says what shipped. Here is how to have an assistant turn it into release posts for the networks your buyers read, queue them for a good hour, and still keep the decision to send in your own hands.

- [Why your assistant rehearses first](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/why-your-assistant-rehearses-first/) - Every write through the Blowhorn MCP server is a dry run until you confirm. What a rehearsal shows, what to check before you say go, why an agent can never approve its own content, and what approval mode and secrets handling add.

- [Ship release notes everywhere your contributors read](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/ship-release-notes-where-contributors-read/) - A tagged release deserves notes written for each place your contributors read: Hacker News, GitHub, Reddit, Slack and Bluesky. Why channel-native wording beats pasting one post everywhere, and how to preview each before it goes out.

- [Let an agent rally the first hour without approving itself](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/rally-the-first-hour-without-approving-itself/) - Plan the first hour of a company post with your teammates' contributed profiles, let an agent queue the work, and keep a person in charge of approval. Why an agent can never approve its own content, and why that is the point.

- [Five minutes from ~/.local/bin/blowhorn-mcp to your first dry run](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/five-minutes-to-your-first-dry-run/) - Open Blowhorn once, add one command to your assistant, check it connected, read something, then rehearse your first write as a dry run and confirm it. Short, exact steps for Claude Code, Cursor, VS Code or Claude Desktop.

- [Agents across client brands, with a kill switch in your hand](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/agents-across-client-brands-kill-switch/) - If you run several brands, an assistant can save hours, and a mistake lands on someone else's name. One profile per client brand, risk limits per profile, a dry run per brand, and a way to pause everything with a reason.

- [Won or lost in the first hour](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/won-or-lost-in-the-first-hour/) - Feeds are widely believed to show a new post to a few people first and widen its reach only if they respond. Here is what that early test looks like, what each platform has and hasn't said about it, and how to schedule so the first hour has readers in it.

- [When a platform changes the rules](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/when-a-platform-changes-the-rules/) - Posting limits shrink, formats change, and yesterday's healthy schedule starts failing. Here is how rule changes show up in your activity, how Blowhorn responds, and the three moves to make on your side.

- [Review the week, not the post](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/tip-review-the-week/) - Single posts are noise. A five-minute weekly review of your activities - success rate, posting times, failure reasons - is where the signal lives, and it is the habit behind every account that keeps improving.

- [A platform limit is information, not an error](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/tip-a-limit-is-information/) - When a platform slows or refuses your posts, it is telling you the current price of attention. The right response is a smaller, slower run - never a faster retry.

- [Read the failure before you retry](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/tip-read-the-failure/) - Every failed post carries its reason, and the reason names the fix. Retrying without reading it repeats the same failure at the same target, and teaches the platform you are not paying attention.

- [Post at your audience's hour, not yours](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/tip-post-at-their-hour/) - Write whenever you think clearly; post when your readers are actually scrolling. Scheduling exists to separate those two moments, and a one-hour shift in posting time is the cheapest experiment in this whole series.

- [Start every new troop on approval](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/tip-approve-first-run/) - Turn on approval for a new troop's first runs. You see each post before it goes out, catch the targeting and wording mistakes while they are still free, then let it run on its own.

- [Space the queue, not the burst](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/tip-space-the-queue/) - If a troop's posts all land inside a few minutes, widen the spacing before you touch anything else. Bursts are the pattern platforms notice, and spacing is the one setting that fixes the shape of a whole run.

- [The scheduling craft: spacing, timing and queues](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/the-scheduling-craft/) - Platforms do not judge your posts one at a time; they judge the shape of your activity. Spacing, timing and queue discipline are how a busy schedule still reads as one person posting at a human pace.

- [Reading your results: what a post's numbers tell you](https://blowhorn.ai/resources/articles/reading-your-results/) - A posted checkmark is the start of the information, not the end. Here is how to read the numbers on a finished activity, spot the three patterns that matter, and change one thing at a time.
